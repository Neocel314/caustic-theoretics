The future arrives wearing clothes we remember. New records sound like recovered tapes. New buildings borrow the vocabulary of vanished industries. New technologies promise to restore the ease of an imagined past.

There is pleasure in repetition, and no reason to pretend otherwise. A familiar sound can return you to a version of yourself you had forgotten. A reissue can rescue something that should never have disappeared.

## Recognition as a default

The difficulty begins when recognition becomes the condition for being heard at all. Something unfamiliar must first explain which familiar thing it resembles. The comparison arrives before the encounter.

This is not evidence that imagination has ended. It is evidence that the routes by which imagination reaches us favour the recognisable. The strange still exists. It is simply harder to find when every recommendation begins with what we already know.

Perhaps the useful question is not why culture repeats itself, but what makes a repetition different. Does it return a possibility to us, or merely return us to a purchase?

I want the kind of return that opens a door. A past approached as unfinished business, rather than a catalogue of reliable effects.
